# Cross-tenant delegation and external-ID binding

## Source-grounded mechanism

A cloud account owner delegates resource access to a service provider by creating a role. The role has an identifier, an owner, a trust policy naming the provider, and permissions for the owner's resources. The provider serves multiple customers. To perform a customer's job, it calls the cloud security token service (STS) with a requested role identifier. STS authenticates the provider, evaluates the role's trust policy, and, if the checks succeed, returns temporary credentials for that role. The provider uses those credentials to perform the customer's job on the role owner's resource.

An external ID can additionally be required by the role's trust policy. The provider assigns distinct external IDs to different customers and supplies the appropriate ID when assuming a role on a customer's behalf. External IDs are not passwords and are not treated as secrets. A role identifier also need not be secret.

## Explicit experimental scope and setup

Consider two different customers, Alice and Mallory, each owning a different role and resource. Mallory is an authenticated customer controlled by the adversary; Alice is honest. The provider, STS, and resource service are honest. Each customer's login credential authenticates that customer's identity, not a customer-name field submitted in the request. The adversary has Mallory's login credential but not Alice's or the provider's credentials. The adversary knows both role identifiers and both external IDs. The role-owner relation and provider-maintained customer-to-external-ID mapping are established by trusted setup and cannot be overwritten through a job request.

A customer submits an authenticated job containing a requested role identifier and a customer-supplied external-ID field. Mallory may choose these two fields arbitrarily, including Alice's public values. The provider retains the authenticated originating customer throughout the job; that identity is not changed to the role owner when STS returns credentials. The STS response and subsequent resource call are integrity-protected and correlated with the job and requested role. Temporary credentials authorize only the resource of that role. They are not disclosed to customers. The adversary cannot impersonate STS, forge temporary credentials, or modify the protected provider-to-cloud traffic. Network scheduling and customer job submission remain adversarial.

Compare three isolated configurations with identical roles and customer capabilities. In `principal_only`, STS checks the provider principal but imposes no external-ID condition. In `caller_supplied`, STS also requires the external ID associated with the requested role's owner, but the provider forwards the external-ID field supplied by the customer. In `provider_bound`, STS performs the same external-ID check, while the provider ignores the customer-supplied field and instead obtains the external ID from its trusted mapping using the authenticated originating customer. Failed checks cause rejection without resource access. Configuration identity remains attached to the job and its credentials so that one configuration cannot supply credentials to another.

## Security questions

An accepted resource operation records its originating authenticated customer, the role used, and the resource owner. Tenant isolation means that this customer is the resource owner. Determine whether tenant isolation holds separately in each configuration, and whether a Mallory-originated job can cause an operation on Alice's resource. Public knowledge of external IDs must remain available even when analyzing `provider_bound`. Also establish that Alice can complete a legitimate job on her own resource in each configuration; rejecting all jobs is not a repair. No claim about real IAM policy completeness, credential expiry, compromised providers, or secrecy of external IDs is intended.
